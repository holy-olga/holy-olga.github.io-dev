## Preamble

I was interested in movement processes that we don’t really call work.

What do I mean by invisible work? It can be very diverse, it can be domestic work or work with children, babies, parenting, and it can apply not only to women, it can be experienced by heterosexual couples, homosexual couples, women and men. Someone is always doing things. Problems are solved, bills are paid, homes tidied up, but what happens when that someone in the background suddenly becomes ill and suddenly it becomes clear how much work they are actually doing? We realise the importance of these things when there is a shortage. Showing the invisible work processes such as making the table, washing the dishes or cleaning the shower cabinet was not an explicit goal of my work, but rather a result.

I worked with the **[MOGI](https://centos.mogi.bme.hu)** department at **BME** where I used their MoCap (motion capture) system (**OptiTrack**) within the **[BME Art Residency](https://rezidensmuvesz.bme.hu)** program. I invited my friends and acquaintances to the movement lab and they did several miscellaneous things: cooked tuna noodles, played the flute, gave a performance, breastfed a child, played with the dog or "curated" (i.e. walking in circles while making a phone call). I converted these 2 - 10 minutes sequences of movements into data using the lab’s special motion capture technology, recorded them on video, took a series of photos and captured them with a **Kinect** camera using our proprietary software. The resulting mappings, or digital data, are displayed in the project in a variety of ways: on projectors, printed images on steel plates, glass objects and 3D printed sculptures.

The artworks on display represent a stage in a continuous and ongoing experiment rather than a conclusive endpoint.

# Try it now

A continuation of Invisible Work which invites exhibition guests to form their own sculptures and captures built from mundane actions they may usually do in their everyday life.

![_parallax(side)](invisible_work/tin-1.webp)

**Invisible Work - Try It Now**, I blend art with state-of-the-art technology to illuminate the unseen labor that underpins our everyday lives. Utilizing three Kinect Azure sensors, the installation captures and tracks viewers’ full-body movements in real time. As participants engage in simple tasks like mopping the floor, their motions are transformed into dynamic, sculptural projections on the surrounding walls. These 3D body stamps not only visualize the choreography of daily actions but also invite viewers to reflect on the hidden efforts that sustain our communities.

Building on my previous research into the performativity of documentation and the interplay between body, space, and time, this interactive installation offers an immersive experience where technology and art converge. By making the invisible visible, Invisible Work - Try It Now highlights the significance of mundane activities and fosters a deeper appreciation for the roles they play in our collective existence. The project challenges viewers to recognize and value the fundamental, often overlooked tasks that maintain the fabric of our social and personal lives, creating a continuous dialogue between the performer and the observer.

**Invisible Work - Try It Now** is an exploration of how everyday gestures can be elevated to artistic expressions, emphasizing the profound impact of what is typically unseen.

## Objectives and Goals

* **Visibility:** To make the invisible labor of everyday tasks visible through artistic expression.
* **Interactivity:** To engage viewers actively, allowing them to become part of the artwork through their own movements.
* **Reflection:** To encourage contemplation on the significance and value of unseen work in societal and personal contexts.
* **Innovation:** To explore the intersection of art and technology, pushing the boundaries of interactive installations.

## Concept and Artistic Vision

Drawing inspiration from phenomenological concepts and posthumanist thought, **Invisible Work - Try It Now** seeks to reframe mundane activities - such as cooking, cleaning, and caregiving - as vital nodes of human interdependency. By capturing these everyday gestures and transforming them into lasting visual forms, the project challenges conventional notions of "labor" and highlights the essential roles these tasks play in our collective existence.

The installation emphasizes the interplay between body, space, and time, creating a continuous dialogue between performer and observer. This hybrid state fosters a deeper understanding of how simple, repetitive actions contribute to the fabric of social cohesion and personal well-being.
